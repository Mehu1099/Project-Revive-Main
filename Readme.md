# Project .revive

**Re-thinking Informality in Informal Settlements**

An interactive web-based portfolio documenting an in-situ slum rehabilitation proposal for Sangam Vihar, one of the largest unauthorized colonies in Asia, located in South Delhi, India.

## About the Project

Sangam Vihar is home to over half a million residents facing significant infrastructure challenges—inadequate water supply, lack of proper sanitation, narrow congested lanes, and insufficient access to basic services. This project recognizes the existing vitality, strong social bonds, and cultural richness of the community while addressing the urgent need for improved living conditions.

Unlike traditional slum rehabilitation approaches that rely on demolition and high-rise construction, **Project .revive** proposes an in-situ upgrading strategy that works *with* the existing urban fabric rather than against it.

## Website Features

- **Dual Navigation Modes**: Story Mode for guided exploration and Explore Mode for free navigation
- **Interactive Image Gallery**: Click-to-focus functionality with categorized content
- **Video Integration**: Embedded video content showcasing site conditions
- **Suggested Path Navigation**: Visual pathways guiding users through the project narrative

## Project Sections

1. **Existing Life & The Challenge** — Understanding the current conditions and community dynamics
2. **Understanding the Site** — Multi-scale site analysis from regional context to neighborhood level
3. **Reality Check & Community Voice** — Community consultations and resident testimonials
4. **The Masterplan** — In-situ upgrading approach with 2D planning and 3D spatial analysis
5. **Housing Infrastructure Upgrades** — Three distinct housing typologies responding to diverse household needs

## Design Approach

The masterplan balances density with quality of life through:
- Slight reconfiguration of existing infrastructure
- Improved housing units with natural light and ventilation
- Strategic interventions preserving community structures
- Locally sourced materials and traditional Indian architectural practices
- Passive systems for sustainability
